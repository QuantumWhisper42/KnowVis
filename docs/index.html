01 · Motivation

When images carry knowledge,
the details matter.

A scientific diagram, a historical scene, or a public sign can look complete while expressing the wrong fact. KnowVis turns these knowledge constraints into visual evidence that can be checked.

01

Implicit constraints

Prompts rarely spell out every physical law, cultural convention, or domain-specific fact needed to draw the scene correctly.

02

Structured expression

Objects must connect through the right relations, formulas, arrows, labels, and text–image bindings.

03

Reliable judgment

Evaluation needs explicit criteria to distinguish a factual error from a valid alternative expression.

03 · Evaluation protocol

Make the judgment inspectable.

Each prompt is decomposed into visual checks. Taxonomy context, knowledge hints, and visual references give the judge concrete evidence for its decisions.

Prompt + image→Structured VQA checklist→PASS / FAIL / NA→Scores + diagnosis

See the knowledge. Inspect the evidence.

Selected checks from each case’s complete checklist.

Selected Required checks · Human annotations

Required

Core correctness

The minimum evidence that the task's core knowledge is correctly expressed. Skill-Tree uses strict case-level correctness; Generalized also measures partial fulfillment.

Optional

Knowledge richness

Predefined supplementary expressions measure how much valid, relevant knowledge appears beyond the core requirement.

Additional

Discover new expressions

Image-derived checks capture supplementary content beyond the current checklist, supporting analysis and future refinement rather than the main richness score.

Keep factual correctness and visual appeal distinct.

A single score cannot say why an image succeeds. The Xanadu case above fails two Required checks, yet its self-added historical figures pass an Optional richness check — wrong core, genuine extra knowledge. Another image could be factually perfect and still lifeless. Generalized evaluation therefore keeps these questions apart:

Is the knowledge right?

Knowledge correctness checks the Required evidence — the porcelain bowl must sit in Kitchenware ∩ Ceramic products. Knowledge richness credits valid extras beyond the core, such as labels naming each painting style. Instruction following covers the non-knowledge brief: five specified items, a Venn layout, the requested language.

Is it well made?

Human raters score image quality (resolution, artifacts, readability of text) and aesthetics (composition, color, style coherence) on the same output. A Rococo hall can pass every knowledge check while its cluttered framing still scores poorly here.

Would people take it?

Overall satisfaction is an independent, task-dependent judgment: given the prompt's actual purpose, would a person keep this image? It correlates with craft and correctness but is decided by neither alone.

05 · Findings

From an overall score
to an actionable diagnosis.

KnowVis exposes differences between rendering objects, expressing their relations, and grounding the scene in the right knowledge.

01

Relations and annotations are harder than objects.

Entity-related checks are generally easier than relational and annotation checks. A model can draw the relevant objects while missing the bindings that make the knowledge correct.
